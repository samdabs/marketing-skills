# Lead finder (free version)

Finds fewer people than paid scraping tools, but each one is researched and worth contacting. Nothing is sent automatically.

## Method
1. Confirm the business and the offer.
2. Describe the ideal customer in 5 lines from real customers: who they are, the problem, where they are, what they already spend, how they search. If there is no customer list, ask for 3 best customers.
3. Find prospects with free sources only: web search, map results, industry and chamber directories, review sites, public posts. Do not log in to any social account and do not use scrapers that break a site's rules.
4. For each prospect record: business, website, a public contact name and role, the one specific thing seen that shows a need (for example few reviews, closed at weekends, no service pages), and the source URL with the date.
5. Score each from 1 to 5 on fit, a visible problem you can fix, a reachable contact, and timing. Sort high to low and show the top 20.
6. Check the law for the recipient's country before any message: CASL in Canada, PECR and GDPR in the UK and EU, CAN-SPAM in the US. Where consent is needed first, use a call, a referral or a public reply instead.
7. Draft each message with one real observation and one free useful thing. No pitch in the first message. A person reviews and sends it.
8. Save the list and log each touch.

## Rules
- Never guess an email address. Use only addresses the person or business published.
- Never invent a detail about a prospect.
- No automatic sending. A person approves every message.
- Include an opt-out line and a real postal address where the law needs them.
- Free first. Ask before spending money.

## Mistakes to avoid
- Long lists with no reason attached to each name.
- Pitching in the first message.
- Bulk sending from your main business domain.
