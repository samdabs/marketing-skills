# Local SEO audit

## Inputs
Business name, website, town, main services, and a link to the Google Business Profile if there is one.

## Method
1. Baseline first. Before changing anything, record where the business shows in the map results for its main service, ideally across several points around the service area. A rank grid tool does this. Without one, search from a few different streets and write down the positions.
2. Address and pin. Confirm the pin sits on the building. Confirm no other profile with the same category shares the address. Note whether the address is hidden or shown.
3. Categories. List the primary and secondary categories. Compare them with the top 3 businesses in the map results for the main service. The primary should match what customers type, such as the problem, not the trade title. Only categories the business really serves.
4. Services. Each category should have its own services listed with a short description, in generic searchable wording.
5. Reviews. Count total reviews, count new ones in the last 90 days, and compare both with the top 3 competitors. Check the average rating and whether the owner replies to every review.
6. Hours. Compare listed hours with when someone really answers the phone. Check holiday hours are set.
7. Phone. Call the listed number in the evening and at the weekend. Note rings, voicemail, or a live answer. Check the number matches the website.
8. Links and listings. Check the chamber of commerce, manufacturer or supplier directories, trade bodies, and any local sponsor pages. Note which link to the website.
9. Photos. Count real photos of the team and jobs, and when the last one was added.
10. Write the fix list, quickest wins first, with exact clicks, time and cost. Free first.
11. Re-check the same grid after two to three weeks. Change one thing at a time and wait a week or two before judging it.

## Rules
- Do not change the name, website address or primary category of a profile that already ranks well without saying it is a risk and getting a yes.
- Never suggest buying reviews, trading reviews, fake clicks or link packages.
- Each finding has a source or a screenshot and a date.
- Note that these signals come from practitioner testing, not from a published ranking formula.

## Output
One page: baseline, what is wrong, ranked fixes, and the date to re-check.
